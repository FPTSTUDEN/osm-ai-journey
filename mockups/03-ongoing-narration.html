The roar of falling water swallows the city noise as you step into the little piazza. Neptune presides over the marble, his sea-horses frozen mid-plunge, and the evening crowd presses close.

Ask the old vendor whether the coin tradition is really true.

The vendor laughs, a dry sound under his grey moustache. “True? Signore, I have sold postcards here forty years — and every face that tosses a coin, I see again. One for the return. Two for love. Three…” he shrugs, “three is for the brave.”

Toss one coin over my shoulder and make the wish I came here with.

You turn your back to Neptune, the way the old rite demands. The coin leaves your fingers, catches a last blade of evening light, and vanishes into the turquoise. The vendor nods once, satisfied, as if a contract has been signed.
